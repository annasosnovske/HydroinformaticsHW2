# Hydroinformatics HW2
Homework 2 - Data Acquisition, Processing, and Communication

![alt text](image.png)

This project analyzed the hydrology of the Island Park Reservoir watershed in eastern Idaho, focusing on the relationship between snowpack accumulation and streamflow to inform reservoir management. Using long term datasets from SNOTEL stations 546 and 680 and USGS streamflow station 13042500, we examined snow water equivalent (SWE) from April through September over the period 1980-2025 and compared this data to corresponding streamflow patterns. Results indicate that SWE peaks in April, with 2025 values slightly below the historical average, while streamflow lags snowmelt, peaking in May through July. The analysis highlights a strong inverse relationship between SWE and early spring streamflow, emphasizing that below-average snowpack on April 1, 2025, corresponds to reduced inflows into the reservoir. These findings suggest that reservoir operators should maintain moderate storage early in the season to optimize water availability while retaining capacity for flood mitigation, demonstrating how integrated SWE and streamflow monitoring supports adaptive water management in snowmelt-driven systems.
